0 · How to read these

Three columns, one contract

Each scenario has the same shape. A trigger: something that happens in your day. Then three columns: what you do, usually one command or one pasted pointer; what the agent reads and writes, always through the operations in CLAUDE.md; and what you get, shown as the answer would actually come back. After that, why you can trust it, which is always the same three mechanisms doing their job: every page says who wrote it and who checked it, every claim carries a footnote to its source, and every answer names its pages with a trust tier and a date.

The tiers, as they appear in answers: unverified the agent wrote it and nobody has checked; machine-confirmed a separate pass re-fetched the source and found the page faithful; human-reviewed you verified it, dated; changed since verified edited after your review, treated as unverified again; stale past its stale_after, whatever the tier. The scenarios reuse the example pages the engine installs, asset search, the review-bot pilot, the media pipeline RFC, so you can run most of them against your own data root today.

S1 · Design

An RFC lands, and you have to review it by Friday

Phase 1 — works nowabout 10 minutes of yoursconnectors: Google Docs or Confluence

Trigger. The media team shares RFC: Media pipeline v2, a Google Doc, and asks for your review. It replaces the nightly batch pipeline with an event-driven one, and asset search consumes its output.

You

Paste the Doc URL into raw/inbox/pull.md. Type your first impressions into a note in raw/inbox/, three lines are enough. Run /pull.

Agent

Checks the Doc is inside the gdocs scope; fetches it read-only. Writes a Source page with a summary, the load-bearing lines as excerpts, and the Doc's modified time. Creates design/rfcs/media-pipeline-v2.md as the entry point: resource → the Doc, options, your notes under Review notes, a Standards check, outcome pending. Links it from systems/asset-search.md. Files a Question if ownership of reconciliation is unclear. Indexes, logs, commits.

You get

An RFC page you can annotate, a query you can ask, and a line on Monday's review under Design — awaiting your decision with the due date.

terminal — after /pull
/query what does media pipeline v2 change for the systems we own, and what does it not address?

answer

Media pipeline v2 replaces the nightly batch rebuild with event-driven writes into the search index. Asset search consumes that index directly, so two things become your concern: how partial events are reconciled before the index is queried, and how a bad batch of events is rolled back. The RFC addresses data retention but not observability or rollback, and it does not say who owns reconciliation.

  • design/rfcs/media-pipeline-v2.md — unverified written by the agent today from the Doc and your notes
  • systems/asset-search.md — human-reviewed 2026-08-26
  • sources/2026-09-02-rfc-media-pipeline-v2.md — machine-confirmed cross-check re-fetched the Doc
  • questions/who-owns-reconciliation.md — unverified filed during ingest

Why you can trust it

Your review notes are yours and say so; everything else on the RFC page is the agent's reading of the Doc, footnoted to the Source page, and marked unverified until you /verify it. The Source page recorded the Doc's modified time, so if the authors edit it before Friday, Monday's drift check reports "changed since read" and queues a re-pull. The canonical RFC stays in the Doc (decision D10); the wiki page is an entry point, never a copy.

You

Agent

Why you can trust it, and where it stops

It only knows what was ingested. A good stand-up card is the return on yesterday's five-minute /ingest of your notes; a teammate's remark in Slack that you never captured is not in it, until the Slack connector arrives in Phase 3. Jira board state is Phase 2 too, so today this covers what you recorded, which for a lead's stand-up is usually the part that matters: decisions, blockers and dates. Each line carries its tier, so "changed since verified" on the System page tells you the KTLO item is the agent's reading of the incident report, not something you have confirmed.

S3 · Delivery

A project meeting in thirty minutes

Phase 1 — works now10 minutes before, 5 afterno connector needed

Trigger. 14:00, review of the review-bot pilot with the Product Trio and Security. You want status, the pending decision, the risks, what each stakeholder has actually said, and to not be caught quoting something the agent guessed.

You

Ask for the brief. Verify the two pages it flags. After the meeting, drop your notes into raw/inbox/ and run /ingest.

Agent

Before: reads the Project page, its linked Decision, the Initiative's Timeline and Stakeholders, open Questions, and the Source pages behind them for the actual words. Flags which of these are unverified. After: updates Status and Next, records the decision's outcome, moves the checkpoint, files the new question, logs, commits.

You get

A one-page brief with a "verify before you quote" line; after the meeting, pages that already say what was agreed.

Why you can trust it

The brief separates what you have checked from what the agent inferred, and says which is which, so decision D4's gate (nothing you say outward rests on an unverified page) is a two-minute step rather than a discipline you have to remember. The quote from Security is an excerpt on the Source page, footnoted, not a paraphrase. After the meeting the accepted decision becomes stable only when you verify the outcome the agent recorded, which is the difference between "we decided" and "the agent heard".

S4 · Team and stakeholders

Slack, two days behind

Phase 3 for the connectorworks now by pasting5 minutes

Trigger. You are back from two days of workshops. #team-search and #incidents have a hundred and forty messages between them, and somewhere in there are three questions only you can answer.

You

Phase 3: /pull "#team-search, last 2 days", named channels only. Today: copy the threads you care about into a note in raw/inbox/ and run /ingest; the rest of the scenario is identical.

Agent

Summarises each thread into a Source page with excerpts and permalinks, never a copy of the thread. Updates Initiative timelines, System incidents and (Phase 3) Stakeholder positions. Then lists what is awaiting you: questions addressed to you or about your systems with no reply from you, requests from stakeholders, decisions someone is waiting on; each with its permalink and a draft reply you can paste. It never posts.

You get

A short list of what needs you, with drafts. You answer in Slack yourself.

awaiting you (3)

1. #team-search, Tue 16:40 — the on-call asks whether the p95 latency SLO should be revisited after the index migration. No reply from you. Draft: "Not before we know whether the breach was the migration or the thresholds; I've filed a question and will bring it to Thursday's review." 2. #incidents, Wed 09:12 — Security asks who to send the data-handling questionnaire to for the review-bot pilot. Draft: "Me, for now; the pilot team owns the harness until the vendor evaluation ends." 3. #team-search, Wed 15:03 — a teammate asks if the KTLO item for the noisy alert can go into this sprint. Draft: "Yes, as the first KTLO item; I've added it to the system page."

  • sources/2026-09-03-team-search-digest.md — unverified 2 threads, 6 participants, excerpts only
  • systems/asset-search.md — changed since verified KTLO section
  • questions/should-the-p95-slo-change.md — unverified new

Why you can trust it, and what it will not do

The pull rule is the guardrail: named channels and threads only, never DMs, never a post, and a Source page holds your reading of a thread rather than the thread, so the vault carries no verbatim Slack. Remarks about people are not recorded; work content is. The drafts are drafts: the agent cannot reply for you, and the design keeps it that way. Until the connector is wired in Phase 3, pasting the threads into a note gives you the same "awaiting you" list, minus the permalinks.

S5 · Systems

An incident on a system you own

Phase 1 — works now5 minutes, then Mondayconnector: Confluence

Trigger. The search-latency alert fired repeatedly on Wednesday night; on Thursday the incident report is in Confluence. As system owner you have to escalate the alert-budget breach and make sure the fix does not get lost.

You

Paste the Confluence URL into pull.md; /pull. Later, one question.

Agent

Source page with the report's key lines. On systems/asset-search.md: unticks the alert-budget item under Operational standards with a footnote, adds the KTLO item, a timeline line, and the incident to Runbooks & links. Updates the Alert budget concept. Files a Question if the cause is unresolved. Lint now reports one unticked standard; Monday's Systems section carries it as an escalation prompt.

You get

A System page that is current, an escalation you cannot forget, and a quarter's worth of incident history on demand.

terminal — Friday
/query what do we know about latency incidents on asset search this quarter, and what changed after each?

answer

Two incidents. 30 July: p95 latency exceeded the SLO for 40 minutes after a bad index rebuild; the rebuild job gained a validation step. 2 September: the latency alert fired repeatedly for three days and breached the alert budget; the on-call attributes it to thresholds set before the index migration, not a regression, and asked for a KTLO item to retune the alert, which is filed but not yet scheduled. One open question: whether the p95 SLO itself should change.

Why you can trust it

The escalation is your own report's words, excerpted and footnoted, on a page that lint marks "changed since verified" until you look at it, which puts it at the top of Monday's queue. The unticked standard is a deterministic finding, not a judgement, and it stays in every lint report until the box is ticked with a footnote of its own. Nothing about the incident lives only in chat memory.

S6 · Design

"Why did we decide that?"

Phase 1 — works now1 minuteno connector needed

Trigger. An engineer who joined last month asks why the pilot extension was gated on a vendor evaluation. Six weeks ago you could have answered from memory; today you want the answer to be the record, not your recollection of it.

You

/query why did we decide to evaluate vendor X before extending the review-bot pilot?

Agent

Reads the Decision page: Context, the three Options, the Decision, Consequences, Standards applied; the Source behind it for the words; the Initiative timeline for what happened since.

You get

The rationale with the alternatives that were rejected, dated and tiered. If it is human-reviewed, you can forward it as is.

answer

Because a vendor shipped an agentic review product the week the pilot was due to extend, and extending first would have meant building on a tool the vendor might make redundant. Three options were considered: extend as planned, evaluate the vendor for two weeks first, or pause the pilot. The second was accepted on 2 September; the cost was a two-week slip of the rollout checkpoint. The vendor-evaluation checklist applied, which is why Security's data-handling review came first.

  • design/decisions/evaluate-vendor-before-extending-pilot.md — human-reviewed 2026-09-02 · status stable
  • sources/2026-08-28-leadership-meeting-notes.md — machine-confirmed
  • delivery/initiatives/ai-assisted-code-review-rollout.md — human-reviewed 2026-09-02

Why you can trust it

A Decision becomes stable only when you verify it, so the answer's first line tells the new engineer whether this is a decision or a proposal. The options that lost are on the page, which is the part memory drops first and the part that stops the same debate from happening twice. If the decision is later superseded, the page stays, marked deprecated with a link to what replaced it, and the answer says so.

Monday, thirty minutes

Phase 1 — works now30 minutesdrift check needs the connectors

Trigger. Monday 8:30. The agent ran over the weekend; the review page is waiting.

You

/weekly if it has not run; open reviews/2026-W37.md in Obsidian; answer inline with →; /weekly --apply.

Agent

Runs lint (script, then the agent pass with source drift), builds the verify queue (five, ranked), the re-pull queue, expiries, checkpoints passed, RFCs awaiting a decision, systems due, open questions, engine proposals. Writes the page. On --apply, executes every answer, logs each as its own label, commits; accepted engine proposals go to the engine repo and its changelog, not the log.

Ten to fifteen decisions, none of which you had to assemble, and a wiki that is still trustworthy next Monday.

Why you can trust it

The queue is capped and ranked, so verification stays a habit rather than a backlog. Expiry forces a choice, refresh, extend or deprecate, so nothing persists silently. And the review is itself a page in the wiki, with a log line for every action it took, so the record of how the wiki was maintained is in the wiki.

9 · Waiting on a later phase

Four more, once their connectors and types exist

Sprint boundary (Phase 2). The sprint-report feed pulls the Jira snapshot; /sprint writes the goal beside what shipped, three numbers with receipts from /measure, deviations with proposed corrective actions, retro notes turned into Playbook proposals, next sprint's goals under the quarter's Objectives. Forty-five minutes, starting from a page instead of a blank Jira report.

Preparing a 1:1 (Phase 3). Monday's review lists each report's last thread and open agreed actions, from their Person page and nothing else, and only if that page is human-reviewed. Two minutes per person, and the page holds only what your own notes said.

Refreshing a technical vision (Phase 4). The Vision page's Signals watched section cites the radar; the quarterly retro asks you to re-read the plan against what the signals said and what shipped, and /brief socialises the result.

A mentoring plan (Phase 4). A Learning Path with owner set to a report: goal, sequence, exit criteria they agreed, progress; prepared with the same loop and reviewed with the same gate.

10 · The four habits

What these scenarios rest on

Every scenario above is a return on one of four small habits, and none works without them. End of day, five minutes: notes into raw/inbox/, /ingest; the stand-up card is only as good as this. Pointers as you meet them: an RFC, a report, a page, into pull.md the moment you see it, not when you need it. Verify what you will quote: /verify --queue before a meeting, five on Monday; the tiers do the rest. Monday, thirty minutes: the review page, answered inline, applied. Skip the first and the wiki goes quiet; skip the third and it fills with the agent's guesses wearing the look of records; skip the fourth and expiry stops meaning anything. The design's promise, Karpathy's, is that the bookkeeping is no longer yours; these four are what remains.

Scenarios reuse the example pages installed by /init --with-examples; names, dates and quotes are illustrative. Commands and tiers follow the engine's CLAUDE.md (0.5.3) and the design page (v0.5).
